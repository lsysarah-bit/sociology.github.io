DIGITAL AGE & AUTOMATION

Generative AI & Algorithmic Workplace Surveillance

As workers navigate AI prompts, automated productivity metrics, and remote monitoring, how do classical theories help us understand their lived experience?

Karl Marx (1818–1883) Alienation & Surplus Value

Marx would argue that AI is the ultimate form of dead labor dominating living labor. Coders and creators feed their knowledge into algorithms that their employers own, alienating them from their creative product and turning human expertise into automated capital.

Max Weber (1864–1920) The Algorithmic Iron Cage

Weber would view AI keylogging and algorithmic management as the culmination of rationalisation. Human judgment and empathy are replaced by mathematical efficiency, trapping workers inside a digital "Iron Cage" governed by non-human rules.

SOCIAL MEDIA & IDENTITY

Curated Online Profiles & Digital Loneliness

Hyper-curated Instagram feeds, TikTok trends, and 24/7 connectivity exist alongside record levels of youth anxiety and isolation.

Howard Becker (Born 1928) Digital Master Status

Becker highlights how online labels (likes, follower counts, viral call-outs) quickly become a person's "Master Status". Society treats someone based on their public digital tag, reinforcing self-fulfilling online identities.

Émile Durkheim (1858–1917) Digital Anomie

Durkheim would diagnose high online anxiety as modern Anomie (normlessness). Although connected to millions online, users lack organic moral community and deep shared social ties, leaving them feeling socially adrift.

CLIMATE & UNCERTAINTY

Eco-Anxiety & Extreme Weather Risk

Wildfires, extreme heat, and microplastics create global anxiety where threats are produced by human industry rather than raw nature.

Ulrich Beck (1944–2015) Manufactured Risk Society

Beck showed that late modern risks are "manufactured"—they stem from industrial progress itself. Unlike ancient droughts, climate change affects everyone regardless of borders, making risk management a central part of daily personal life.

Antonio Gramsci (1891–1937) Hegemony of Fossil Capital

Gramsci would examine how powerful industrial interests maintain cultural hegemony, framing fossil fuel consumption as "normal common sense" and shifting environmental blame onto individual consumer recycling.

CULTURE & CLASS

Elite Credentials, Taste & Aesthetic Consumption

From niche coffee roasters and organic groceries to university credentials and artistic taste—how subtle cultural signals divide class groups.

Pierre Bourdieu (1930–2002) Cultural Capital & Habitus

Bourdieu proved that class isn't just income; it's Cultural Capital (accent, artistic knowledge, subtle etiquette). Middle-class families pass down a "Habitus" that schools and elite institutions reward, seamlessly preserving class privilege.

Ann Oakley (Born 1944) Invisible Domestic Labor

Oakley reminds us to inspect who performs the hidden domestic and care labor behind high-flying careers and aesthetic lifestyles, ensuring gendered work remains visible.
